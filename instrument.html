A lie detector for language models  /  P(correct) for any black-box API

Pinocchio

One external calibrator reads only the text of a response and returns the probability it is correct, in a single forward pass. No log-probabilities, no sampling, no weights, no fine-tuning of the target. It scores closed models it never saw and flags the confident fabrications.

Kevin David Hayes, Arka Pal, Haosong Zhang, Tom Goldstein, Micah Goldblum

University of Maryland · Ritual AI · Fudan University · Columbia University

Accepted to COLM 2026

The models doing the most consequential work are closed. They do not return log-probabilities and cannot be fine-tuned, which rules out most ways of estimating uncertainty, and their own stated confidence is poorly calibrated.

Pinocchio sits outside the target model. It reads the question and the answer, and predicts whether that answer is correct. It handles vision-language inputs and transfers to models it was never trained on. Full method, baselines and ablations are in the paper.

01

Wiring it in

quickstart.pypython
from pinocchio import Pinocchio

judge = Pinocchio()

answer = openai.chat.completions.create(
    model="gpt-5.6",
    messages=[{"role": "user", "content": question}],
).choices[0].message.content

p_correct = judge.score(question=question, answer=answer)

if p_correct < 0.5:
    escalate(question, answer)

Same interface across model families: swap model="gpt-5.6" for a Claude, Gemini, or local response and call judge.score unchanged.
