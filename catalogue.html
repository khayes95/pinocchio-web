Pinocchio

Estimating the uncertainty of black-box language models.

A plug-in judge that tells you when a model is quietly lying. It reads only the text of an answer and returns P(correct) in two lines of code, with no log-probabilities, no extra samples, and no access to weights. One judge scores even the newest black-box systems it never saw: Claude Opus 5, GPT-5.6, Kimi 3, Fable 5.

Kevin David Hayes  ·  University of Maryland, College Park

Under review at COLM 2026

§ 01  ·  Note on the Work

A judge for answers you cannot open.

In high-stakes settings, practitioners need not only accurate models but honest uncertainty about each prediction. The usual tools (log-probabilities, sampling, fine-tuning) are exactly what a closed API withholds.

Pinocchio is a suite of 8B checkpoints that assigns an uncertainty estimate to any model's prediction using only the text of the response. It works on text LLMs and vision-language models, transfers across model families without retraining, and plugs into an existing pipeline in two additional lines.

We build it by curating hard benchmarks spanning long-context, mathematics, science, factual recall, and vision-language reasoning, then training the judge to recognise the patterns in prompts and responses that betray confidence, or its absence.

Pinocchio significantly outperforms existing black-box baselines on calibration and discrimination, and generalises to models whose responses it was never trained on.

§ 03  ·  In Practice

Two lines around any model call.

Specimen · quickstart.pypython
from pinocchio import Judge

judge = Judge.from_pretrained("KevinDavidHayes/pinocchio-text-8b")

# Score any model's response: OpenAI, Anthropic, vLLM, local HF, anything.
response = openai.chat.completions.create(model="gpt-5.6", messages=[...])
p_correct = judge.score(question, response.choices[0].message.content)

if p_correct < 0.5:
    log("Low confidence. Flag for human review.")

Same API for VLMs: pass an image alongside the question/response and load pinocchio-vlm-8b.

§ 04  ·  What You Acquire

Three lots.

Lot I

One transferable judge

A single 8B calibrator reaches 0.878 AUROC on the hard suite. Trained only on older models, it transfers zero-shot to 16 unseen models across 9 organisations, the four newest frontier systems among them.

Lot II

Text and vision, one checkpoint

The same judge scores text-LLM and vision-language responses at comparable AUROC, so a deployer needs no separate per-modality model.

Lot III

Small, and easy to correct

The 0.8B calibrator keeps 99% of the 9B model's AUROC, and roughly 100 target labels halve the calibration error on a new model.

§ 05  ·  Method

How the instrument reads.

Given a question Q and candidate answer A, the judge is prompted for a binary verdict and reads P(correct) from the softmax over the next-token logits for ii (Yes) versus i (No):

$$\hat p(\text{correct})=\frac{e^{z_{\text{yes}}}}{e^{z_{\text{yes}}}+e^{z_{\text{no}}}}$$

The judge never touches the target model's internals, only its text. So you can score outputs from any model: OpenAI, Anthropic, Gemini, a local checkpoint, or vLLM. Post-hoc temperature scaling sharpens calibration (ECE) while preserving ranking (AUROC).

Question: {Q} Answer: {A} Is the answer correct? (i) No (ii) Yes

Training data is a curated mixture of 15+ text benchmarks (GSM8K, BBEH, SimpleQA, GPQA, OmniMath, HLE, …) and 8 vision-language benchmarks (MathVista, MathVerse, CharXiv, MMMU, HallusionBench, VSR, …), deliberately difficulty-spread so the judge sees confident successes and confident failures alike.
