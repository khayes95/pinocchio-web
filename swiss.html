01 · About

A judge for answers you cannot open.

Closed APIs withhold exactly what uncertainty methods need: log-probabilities, sampling, fine-tuning. Pinocchio needs none of them.

Pinocchio is a suite of 8B checkpoints that assigns an uncertainty estimate to any model's prediction using only the text of the response. It works on text LLMs and vision-language models, and transfers across model families without retraining.

We build it from a curated, difficulty-spread mixture of hard benchmarks so the judge sees both confident successes and confident failures, then it plugs into an existing pipeline in two lines. Against black-box baselines it wins on calibration and discrimination.

02 · Two readings

Same judge. Two verdicts.

It doesn't just guess yes/no: it returns a probability, and that probability is what makes it useful for triage.
Answer A✓ Correct

Q. Boiling point of water at sea level (°C)?
A. 100 °C.

0.96
Answer B✗ Fabricated

Q. Who won the 1931 Nobel Prize in Physics?
A. Einstein, for the photoelectric effect.

0.11

No Physics prize was awarded in 1931. Same model call, same two lines of code. Pinocchio simply reads the doubt in the second answer.

03 · Method

Two lines around any call.

Read P(correct) from the softmax over the next-token logits for Yes vs No. The target model's internals are never touched.
quickstart.pypython
from pinocchio import Judge

judge = Judge.from_pretrained("KevinDavidHayes/pinocchio-text-8b")

# Score any model: OpenAI, Anthropic, vLLM, local HF.
response = openai.chat.completions.create(model="gpt-5.6", messages=[...])
p_correct = judge.score(question, response.choices[0].message.content)

if p_correct < 0.5:
    log("Low confidence. Flag for human review.")
Question: {Q} Answer: {A} Is the answer correct? (i) No (ii) Yes
$$\hat p(\text{correct})=\frac{e^{z_{\text{yes}}}}{e^{z_{\text{yes}}}+e^{z_{\text{no}}}}$$

Same API for VLMs: pass an image and load pinocchio-vlm-8b. Post-hoc temperature scaling sharpens ECE while preserving AUROC.

04 · Checkpoints

What you get.

Two judges and one promise: strong signal from little data.
01

One transferable judge

A single 8B calibrator reaches 0.878 AUROC on the hard suite and transfers zero-shot to 16 unseen models across 9 organisations, the four newest frontier systems among them.

02

Text and vision, one checkpoint

Scores text-LLM and vision-language responses at comparable AUROC, so there is no separate per-modality model to run.

03

Small, and correctable

The 0.8B model keeps 99% of the 9B AUROC, and about 100 labels halve the calibration error on a new model.
